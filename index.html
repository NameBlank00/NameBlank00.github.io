About Me

Physics and mathematics graduate of the University of Houston.

Hi, I'm Jonathan

As an undergraduate researcher I studied how resonance decays affect the susceptibilities of conserved quantities, and compared the results with published experimental data.

My main interest is theoretical physics, especially theoretical high-energy physics, along with quantum information theory.

Mathematics is the language; physics is the story.

This site collects what I'm studying and working on: research interests, a reading list of textbooks, and course-style notes running from the foundations of mathematics to quantum field theory.

Research Interests

Modeling the thermodynamic behavior of Quantum Chromodynamics, and the mathematical structures that make physical theories precise.

Particle Physics

The fundamental particles and their interactions across energies, temperatures and densities, from collider experiments to the early Universe.

QCD Thermodynamics

Susceptibilities of conserved charges and the role of resonance decays, using approaches such as:

  • Lattice QCD
  • Hadron Resonance Gas model
  • Nambu–Jona-Lasinio model
  • Holographic models

Mathematical Physics

New physics motivates new mathematics, and that mathematics in turn gives physics a rigorous framework and new insight. I'm also interested in quantum information theory.
